Chapter 2 — Anchoring

Why comments survive edits, and where they don't.

The stored anchor

When you comment, the layer stores three strings: the exact text you selected, a slice of what came before it, and a slice of what came after. It never stores a DOM path or a character offset into the whole document, because both of those break the moment anyone edits the page.

Re-finding the text

On load, the layer scans the page for the exact quote. If the quote appears only once, that's the anchor. If it appears several times — imagine the word "delivery" repeated across a section — it scores each occurrence by how much of the stored before-and-after context still matches, and highlights the best one. This is the same repeated word, resolved by its surroundings.

When an anchor is dropped

If the exact quoted text no longer exists anywhere on the page, the layer can't place the highlight, so it quietly skips it. The comment isn't deleted — it still shows up in the export — it just has nowhere visible to attach. Rewrite the quoted words and you sever the anchor; edit around them and it holds.
